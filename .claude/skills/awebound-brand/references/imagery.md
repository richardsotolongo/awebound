# Illustration and product images

## Apparel art

- Symbols only (see `faith-content.md`). One central subject, framed by botanicals, architecture, weather or light.
- Engraved line work with crosshatched shading; bold lettering is welcome (engraved capitals, heavy gothic capitals, ornate script). No flat clip art, 3D renders or photo collage.
- Inks: bone, black and deep red, with the occasional single accent ink (Stone in Motion's cobalt, By His Hem's rust).
- The wordmark stays whole: above, below or inside the scene, never cut by it. The Thorn Cross may sit beside or below it.
- The reference or the verse in approved wording sits at the base or above the scene. Record exactly what's printed, and in which translation, in the piece's `story.back`.

## Product images on the site

- Images show **the item exactly as Fourthwall will make it** and follow each product's image list in Fourthwall, in the same order; the first is the listing image. Tees and hoodies have four images, caps three. Every image of a piece shares its surface: Fourthwall's finished photos are shown as they are (cropped to 4:5), with each piece's surface matched to its photos' background. Every garment sits at the same scale: a photo framed tighter than the rest (Stone in Motion's two full views, whose sleeves run off the frame) carries a `frame` in `fourthwall.json`, and `widen.py` zooms it out to the other tees' scale: the garment is cut from the photo with its own shadow and its sleeve tips completed from the sleeve's own fabric, and the whole background is made fresh from the photo's own light and grain (quilted from its garment-free patches), so it is even from edge to edge. The print is never touched. A photo that shows the print wrongly is left out (listed under `holds`) until it is replaced in Fourthwall, with Fourthwall's cut-out render of the correct print on the surface instead. A piece not yet in Fourthwall uses a clean cutout of the approved mockup.
- Each garment sits on its own surface with a soft, realistic shadow and enough contrast to set it apart. The set shares the 4:5 canvas, margins and garment scale (tees about three quarters of the width), so the grid reads as one family; each surface is a color drawn from the piece's artwork, with its own texture and light.
- Behold surfaces: Still the Storm, deep storm slate with cloud-soft texture lit from above; By His Hem, muted sage plaster (the rust print's complement); Thorns to Lilies, deep oxblood plaster under a spotlight; Torn Veil, deep crimson plaster lit from above; Stone in Motion, dusty cornflower stone with a fine speckle; To Live Is Christ, warm ochre plaster lit from above; The Passage, pale sandstone plaster lit from the upper left; Wonderfully Made, dusty rose paper lit from above; Lamb's Mark, warm linen lit from the upper left; Signature Cap, warm sand concrete with a fine speckle lit from above.
- Every piece also has a close-up of its artwork (`detail.webp` beside its images), cropped from its Fourthwall render (or from one of its photos when the print changed after the render, as for The Passage): the whole print on its fabric and nothing else, never enlarged, retouched or recolored. The home page shows the lead piece’s, and each product page shows its own in the design story. It is not part of the product gallery.
- Every piece has a **plate**: its surface alone as a wide and a tall field (`plate-wide.webp`, `plate-tall.webp`), lit where the garment sits and a little darker (bone lettering) or lighter (coal lettering) where words go, and the listing image's garment cut out with its shadow (`piece.webp`), cut by `photo_cutout.py` from the shop's first photo itself with that photo's own shadow (a tee's sleeves that run off the photo are completed; a hoodie's fade out at the edge), so the home page shows exactly what the shop leads with. The home page sets the cut-out and the tagline on the lead piece’s plate; each product page sets the piece’s call on its plate. Nothing but lettering of 24px and up, filled buttons and images goes on a plate.
- The product stays the focal point: preserve the artwork and garment colors exactly; the listing image always shows the whole garment; artwork crops are secondary; no props or textures over the print; no fog, dramatic effects, glows or white outlines; clean garment edges. Changing the page color doesn't fix an embedded white background: cut the garment out and composite it.
- In the repo: `assets/mockups/fourthwall.json` lists each product's Fourthwall images in order and `fourthwall.py` downloads them; `assets/mockups/source` holds the approved mockups (keep them intact) and `cutout.py` cuts them out; `fourthwall.json` also keeps each piece's current renders under `cutouts`, for the home page cut-out and the close-up; `compose.py` builds the site images (`apps/web/public/products/<slug>/1.webp`, `2.webp`, …), each piece’s close-up (`detail.webp`; the crop is set per piece in `VIEWS`) and its plate (surface in `STYLE`, shading in `PLATE_SHADE`), and prints the contrast each plate gives bone and coal lettering.
