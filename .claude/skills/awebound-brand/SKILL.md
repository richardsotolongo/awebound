# Awebound brand

Awebound is an independent Christian apparel brand, founded by Richard out of his faith and reverence for Jesus Christ. Awebound makes clothing rooted in Scripture: every piece tells part of His story in bold art and lettering. The range covers tees, oversized tees, hoodies, tank tops and hats, with more to come.

**The store welcomes everyone.** Someone who doesn’t share Richard’s faith should still find the clothes beautiful, understand where they come from and buy comfortably. The site says what Awebound makes and why. It never tells a visitor what to believe, assumes they believe, or says who the clothes are “for”.

Tagline: **Bound in awe. Worn without shame.** (Romans 1:16 is behind the second half.)

The look is a dark coal shop with a hint of dark metal: layered near-blacks, sharp edges and gothic lettering at poster size, cut by full-bleed **plates** (each piece's own colored surface, with the garment on it) and by warm-bone panels that carry the oxblood wordmark. Sections meet edge to edge; there are no glows, gradients or rounded panels. The test for every piece of work is that a pastor and a metalhead should both find it beautiful.

**This guide matches the live site (October 2026).** The implementation is `packages/brand` (tokens, components, marks) and `apps/web` (pages, content) in the `richardsotolongo/awebound` repo, and `docs/BRAND.md` there is a copy of this file. Paths starting `assets/`, `references/` or `scripts/` are relative to the skill folder (`.claude/skills/awebound-brand`), not the repo root. The tokens, component files, Tailwind files, logos and marks under `assets/` are copied or built from `packages/brand`; when the site's brand code changes, refresh them (see "Keeping this skill in sync").

## Non-negotiables

Break one and the work is off-brand no matter how good it looks.

1. **The wordmark is artwork, never type.** Use the wordmark SVG (`assets/logos/awebound-wordmark.svg`, single path, `currentColor`) or the `Wordmark` component. Never set AWEBOUND in a font, never split the lettering from its thorn branches, and never claim a website font is its typeface. Minimum 120px wide on screen; smaller than that, use the small Thorn Cross.
2. **Signature lockup = oxblood `#782B35` on warm bone `#E8DDC9`** for brand moments (hang tags, labels, packaging, About page, email headers, avatar). On the dark site the header uses bone on coal.
3. **Crosses stay upright and whole.** The Thorn Cross (a two-plank wooden cross with a thorn wreath orbiting at 45°) is the brand mark. Release artwork may carry its own simple cross (inside the wreath in Thorns to Lilies, in the hourglass in To Live Is Christ, Wonderfully Made's wooden cross). Never tilted, broken or underfoot; no budded crosses, no four-point stars, no crucifix figure.
4. **Symbols only.** A stilled sea, the edge of His cloak, thorns and lilies, the torn veil, the empty tomb and rolled stone, the hourglass, a path through a dark valley, roses around a cross, the Lamb, broken chains, arches. The Lamb is a biblical symbol, not a portrait. The hand in By His Hem is the woman's, reaching for His cloak; never draw Christ. Never depict Jesus (face, silhouette, hands), God the Father or the Spirit as a person; no gore, skulls or occult symbols.
5. **Scripture is quoted exactly.** See "Scripture" below: full references, NIV wording on the website, labels that say when a quote is an excerpt, and printed garment lettering left as approved.
6. **Internal design codes stay internal.** IDs such as A3-B01 or A3-H01 live in records, SKUs, assets and orders. Never show them to customers: not on cards, product headings, badges, related pieces, search results, bag lines or metadata. Customers know a piece by its name, artwork and Scripture.
7. **Say "Christian apparel", never "streetwear".** Use Christian apparel, clothing, pieces or Awebound, in headings, descriptions, About, FAQ and page metadata. Fit words such as "oversized" belong in product details and sizing.
8. **Oxblood is a fill, not text on dark** (1.9:1 on coal). Accent text on dark is Copper 300 `#C47A55`; Scripture references are gold `#B89651`.
9. **Tokens, not hex.** In code, style through the CSS variables (`packages/brand/src/styles/tokens.css` in the repo, `assets/tokens.css` elsewhere). Hex values appear only in token files (and in HTML email, where clients ignore CSS variables).

## Scripture

- **References**: full book name, chapter, colon, verse: "Matthew 9:21", "1 Corinthians 3:6", ranges with an en dash.
- **Website quotations use the NIV**, word for word with its punctuation, from an authorized NIV source. Never paraphrase and present it as a quote; never invent or "tidy" wording. An excerpt ends where the quoted part ends, without added punctuation.
- **Label** every quotation with the reference, then the translation: `Mark 4:41 — NIV`; when only part of the verse is quoted, `Matthew 9:21 — NIV, excerpt`.
- **Presentation**: the words in Cormorant Garamond Italic inside curly double quotes; quotation marks inside the verse become single quotes (“They asked each other, ‘Who is this?’”). The reference sits beneath in upright type. Quotes wrap in full on every screen: never truncate with an ellipsis or shrink them to equalize card heights.
- **One shared Scripture record per piece** (reference, excerpt flag, exact NIV and KJV text) feeds the home page, shop cards and product page, so they always agree. In the site this is `scripture` in `apps/web/src/server/content/catalog.json`; site-wide verses live in `verses.json`. `SCRIPTURE_TRANSLATION` (NIV by default, or KJV) switches every quotation and translation statement at once.
- **Garment artwork keeps its approved lettering.** Some prints use King James wording (Still the Storm's back, Stone in Motion's back); By His Hem's back prints NIV wording, which needs Biblica's merchandise permission. Never translate, regenerate or relabel printed art as NIV; describe printed text accurately ("Mark 4:41 in the King James wording"), and keep image descriptions true to what the image shows.
- **Permission and notice**: website use of the NIV needs Biblica's written permission (biblica.com/permissions) before launch, and every page that quotes it carries the notice (the site puts it in the footer): "Scripture quotations taken from The Holy Bible, New International Version® NIV®. Copyright © 1973, 1978, 1984, 2011 by Biblica, Inc.™ Used by permission. All rights reserved worldwide." Use the exact wording Biblica supplies. Merchandise that prints NIV text needs its own permission.
- About and FAQ state the website translation plainly: "Scripture quoted on this website is from the New International Version (NIV)."
- In brand prose, pronouns for God are capitalized (His mercy, He is risen). Quotations keep their translation's own casing.

## Typography

Three families, each with one job. Load only the files used, self-hosted, with fallbacks.

| Role               | Family                                                    | Use                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Display            | **Grenze Gotisch** (variable, 600)                        | Lettering at poster size: the tagline, release names (“Behold” set to fill the width), each piece’s call on its plate, page titles. Always mixed case: blackletter capitals are hard to read. Sized so its longest line always fits; never stretched or outlined. |
| Scripture          | **Cormorant Garamond Italic 500** (a genuine italic file) | Quotations only. About 23px beside a product name, 19px in cards, up to 36px for the one featured verse on a page; no added letter spacing; line height about 1.4.                                                                                                |
| Body and interface | **Manrope** (variable)                                    | Paragraphs (16–18px, generous line height), navigation, form labels, buttons, prices, filters, forms, sizing, shipping, Scripture references, and product names (800 weight, caps, slight tracking; up to 60px on the product page).                              |

Avoid gothic type for paragraphs, sizing, numbers and roman numerals (they turn to blackletter shapes; set numerals in Manrope); stretching or compressing any font to imitate the wordmark; distressed effects; heavy letter spacing in Scripture; a fourth decorative face.

## Releases and the shop

- A **release** (a collection in code) is a small set of pieces drawn from one call in Scripture, numbered in order: Release 01 is **Behold** (five oversized tees, three hoodies and two caps): "Clothes that ask you to look at Jesus, and keep looking." The name comes from John 1:29 (older English Bibles begin John's words with "Behold"; the NIV says "Look").
- **Latest Drop** always means the newest release with pieces on the site, worked out from the catalog, never hard-coded. Say "Latest Drop", not "New Collection".
- The shop keeps its choices compact: a short search box, then one row of three selectors, **Collection** first (All collections, the default, then each release by name: Behold for now), then **Category** (All products, Tees, Oversized tees, Hoodies, Hats) and Sort (Featured first), with Filters at the end. On All collections the shop's header promotes the Latest Drop, so the newest release is what Shop leads with as releases are added. No rows of tabs or counts. An empty state says what is missing with a reset, and URLs stay shareable (`/shop?collection=behold&category=hats`; `/collections/<slug>` opens the shop filtered to it).
- The main navigation is Shop, About, Contact, with the account link (Sign in, or **Account** once signed in) and the bag beside it. While there is one release, Collections is the shop (`/collections` opens it), so it is not in the navigation. The invitation is **Join Awebound**; inside the account, **My Archive** (the overview) and **Your collection** (where each bought design's story, Richard's note and extras live) are the two red buttons, then a rule and Saved, Orders, Settings, and the overview needs no other title. The overview's first-visit welcome is one sentence and one next step (“Welcome to your Archive.”, “Save pieces with the heart. Each design you buy opens here with its deeper story, a founder’s note from Richard and its wallpapers and extras.”, **Explore Behold**). **Your collection** shows only the designs the account owns, one card each with its link at the card's foot; empty, it says “Your collection starts with your first piece.”, lists what each design brings to it as short dotted lines (the deeper story behind the design, the meaning of its imagery, a founder’s note from Richard, its Scripture and biblical context, phone and desktop wallpapers, a design card PDF and story images to share), says why it is empty (no order linked to the account yet, or purchases not linked to accounts yet) and one next step (**Explore [release]**). A design's own page, for someone who doesn't own it: “Own this design to unlock its full story and founder note.” Only what is ready shows: a wallpaper appears once published, with no "being prepared" line, and the Archive has no care section (care belongs to the product, not the Archive). No Founding counts, perks or countdowns in public copy, and never a fictional or empty "coming soon" collection.
- **Ready to order, everywhere.** The site always reads as open for ordering: no release-status sentences, no “coming soon”, no preview notices and no “ordering isn’t open” anywhere (the owner’s choice, so the site can be shown and marketed while the last back-end steps are finished). A piece speaks only about stock: “Sold out” or “Some sizes sold out”. Never announce a launch date.
- **Unpublished pieces and the bag.** A piece that isn’t published in Fourthwall yet looks exactly like one on sale: its price, sizes, **Add to bag**, and a bag with **Check out**. Only checkout tells them apart: it can’t start for an unpublished piece, so the bag says “Checkout isn’t available right now. Your bag is saved, so please try again a little later.” and stays as it is. A selection saved before a piece is published carries over.
- **The supplier is never named to customers.** Copy says “our print and fulfillment partner” or “our checkout partner”, so the supplier can change without rewriting the site.

## Presenting a piece

- **Card** (shop): the whole garment showing its main artwork, on its own color with no panel behind the words; the name first; garment type and base price on one row beneath it (no “From”); a plain stock line when one applies (“Sold out”, “Some sizes sold out”); the NIV quote and its label. The card links to the product page. Nothing sits above the name, and there is no color subtitle ("Tee · Faded black") and no code. Every card and tile has a **save heart** in the bottom right corner of its image: an outline in bone on a translucent coal square, filled once saved; signed out, it leads to sign-in. The product page has the same heart beside Add to bag. With a mouse, hovering shows the piece's other side; on a touch screen a sideways swipe across the image moves through all of the piece's images without opening it (small dots in the bottom left say where you are), and a tap still opens it.
- **Wall tile** (home, “More from Behold”, About): the same card without the quote, because a tile is too narrow to quote a verse in full and verses are never shortened. Tiles meet with no gaps, two across on phones, three from 600px, five from 1100px; a short last row is filled by a tile that links to the whole release.
- **Product page**, top to bottom: the stage (the product’s images, filling the left of the page on desktop with thumbnails beneath; swipe on phones) beside the breadcrumb, the name, garment type and price on one row, the quote and its label (not repeated lower down), color (name beside the swatch) and size with a size guide, Add to bag, **delivery and returns** in brief, and a short My Archive note (“Your piece continues in My Archive.” and “Purchase this design to unlock the deeper story behind it, a founder’s note from Richard and digital extras like phone and desktop wallpapers, kept with its Scripture and biblical context.”, with one quiet link: **Create your free Archive** signed out, **Open My Archive** for a member, **View this piece in My Archive** for an owner); then the design story on the piece’s plate (its call, such as “Behold His mercy”, as lettering beside a close-up of the print), what the art shows and what it means on coal; **Product details** (garment, fit, color, front, back, inks or thread, fabric); the previous and next pieces by image and name; and the rest of the release as a wall. On phones the price and the bag button stay in a bar at the bottom once the button has scrolled away.
- **Delivery and returns summary** beside the buy button: made to order; production time and shipping time stated separately; how shipping is charged (calculated at checkout for the address, shown before paying); links to the size guide and the returns and refunds policy; the difference between a problem with an order (damage, a misprint or defect, or the wrong item or size sent, reported within 30 days of delivery with a photo: replaced at no cost, or refunded if a replacement isn't possible) and a size the customer chose that doesn't fit (made to order, so no returns or exchanges for it). Times are Fourthwall’s standard ones for made-to-order pieces (2–5 business days to produce; 3–7 business days to ship in the US, 2–4 weeks elsewhere), set in `apps/web/src/lib/delivery.ts`.
- Selected size and color always show in the bag and order summary; cut, fit and fabric in Product details; measurements and sizing advice in the size guide.
- **Size guide**: every piece shows Awebound's own size guide image for its blank, with a one-line fit summary above it: the Middleweight Oversized T-shirt (Thorns to Lilies, By His Hem, Stone in Motion), the Organic Oversized T-shirt (To Live Is Christ, Still the Storm), the Premium Relaxed Hoodie (all three hoodies), the Low-Profile Adjustable Cap (Lamb's Mark) and the Mid-Profile Snapback Cap (Signature Cap). No “size up” advice: the owner wore the Organic Oversized in his usual size. Never name the blank's brand to customers.
- **Returns** follow Fourthwall's policy everywhere (refunds page, FAQ, product page, terms, llms.txt): damage, misprints, defects, and the wrong item or size sent, reported within 30 days of delivery with a photo, are replaced at no cost or refunded if a replacement isn't possible; no returns or exchanges for a size the customer chose or a change of mind; expected muting of colors printed on dark garments isn't a defect; lost packages after 2 weeks without tracking (3 international); changes and cancellations only before production; EU, UK and EEA customers keep their 14-day right of withdrawal.

## Home page order

1. **The opening**, on a piece’s plate: the headline “Bound in awe. Worn without shame.” at poster size with no label above it, two buttons (**View [piece]** to its product page and **See all 10 pieces** to the shop) and the garment cut out beside them. Every visit opens on the release's primary piece (its featured one: Thorns to Lilies in Behold), then the pieces take turns every seven seconds while the plate is on screen: the plate cross-fades, and the garment fades out before the next one fades in, never two at once; small Previous, Pause and Next buttons sit under the two buttons, and on a touch screen a sideways swipe across the plate moves to the next or previous piece (the garment follows the finger a little while it moves). On phones the words and buttons come first, then the garment: the first button must be on the first screen.
2. **On coal beneath it**: the brand paragraph ("Awebound makes clothing rooted in Scripture and reverence for Jesus Christ. Every piece tells part of His story in bold art and lettering, from the storm He calmed to the tomb He left empty.") and the piece on the plate up close: a close-up of its print with one sentence on what the art shows, its name, price and NIV quote. It follows the plate as the pieces take turns, and the plate holds still while a pointer or focus rests on it.
3. **Richard’s note**, on bone paper: his portrait, the heading “My faith in God comes first.”, his name, his two sentences and **Read my story**.
4. **The release**: its name across the width, its one-line call and reference, then the wall of its pieces in order.
5. **My Archive**, on coal: the heading “Wear the piece. Know its story.”, one paragraph (“My Archive is your personal space for the Awebound pieces you love. Create a free account to save your favorites. Each design you buy unlocks its deeper story, a personal note from Richard and digital extras like phone and desktop wallpapers, all kept with its Scripture and biblical context, bringing you closer to the meaning behind what you wear.”) and one outlined button (**Create your free Archive**, or **Open My Archive** for a member), beside a preview of one design's card: the garment on its plate as an image only, then its name, Scripture reference and published public preview on raised coal (until a design is published, the lead piece's own name, reference and the sentence on what its art shows), the parts a purchase opens named with a small lock (never shown: the story, the meaning of its imagery, Richard's note, wallpapers, the design card PDF and story images), and the caption “A preview of the [piece] card in My Archive.” On phones the words and button come first, then the card.

There is no release-updates sign-up for now, on the home page or in the footer.

What every page leaves out on purpose: labels above headings, status chips, glows, gradients, and any animation on load or on scroll. **Motion is one idea**: a piece’s listing image travels from its tile or card into its product page, neighbouring pieces slide in from their side, and pages change with a short cross-fade. Swipes follow the finger: a card's images scroll sideways under it, and the opening plate's garment moves a little before the next piece shows. The one exception is the home page’s opening plate, where the pieces take turns: the plate cross-fades, and the garment fades out before the next one fades in, so two garments never show at once. It moves only while the plate is on screen, never while a pointer rests on its buttons, its garment or the piece up close below it, or focus is inside them, with a Pause button, and never with reduced motion (then every change is instant). Artwork, Scripture and buttons are whole and readable in every frame (never cropped, wiped or faded in), anchors land below the sticky header, and reduced-motion settings get instant navigation. Test on phones as well as desktop.

## Imagery and product photos

- Product images show **the item exactly as Fourthwall will make it**, and follow each product's image list in Fourthwall, in the same order: the first is the listing image. Tees and hoodies have four images, caps three. Fourthwall's finished photos are shown as they are (cropped to 4:5), and each piece's surface is matched to its photos' background, so every image of a piece shares one background. Every garment sits at the same scale: Fourthwall's photos are framed alike (Stone in Motion's were reframed in Fourthwall in October 2026 to match the others and are shown as they are). A photo framed tighter than the rest is zoomed out to the others' scale: the garment is cut from the photo with its own shadow and its sleeve tips completed from its own fabric, on a background made fresh from the photo's own light and grain so it is even from edge to edge, never touching the print. A photo that shows the print wrongly is left out until it is replaced in Fourthwall, with Fourthwall's cut-out render of the correct print on the surface in its place. A piece not yet in Fourthwall uses a clean cutout of the approved mockup. Each garment sits on its own surface, with a soft realistic shadow and enough contrast to set the garment apart. The set shares the 4:5 canvas, margins and garment scale (tees about three quarters of the width) so the grid reads as one family; each piece gets its own surface, a color drawn from its artwork with its own texture and light, so every piece looks distinct.
- Behold surfaces: Still the Storm, deep storm slate with cloud-soft texture lit from above; By His Hem, muted sage plaster (the rust print's complement); Thorns to Lilies, deep oxblood plaster under a spotlight; Torn Veil, deep crimson plaster lit from above; Stone in Motion, dusty cornflower stone with a fine speckle; To Live Is Christ, warm ochre plaster lit from above; Fear Not, pale sandstone plaster lit from the upper left; Wonderfully Made, warm rose paper lit from above; Lamb's Mark, warm linen lit from the upper left; Signature Cap, warm sand concrete with a fine speckle lit from above.
- Every piece has a close-up (`detail.webp` beside its images): a crop of the artwork from its Fourthwall render (or from a photo when the print changed after the render, as for Fear Not), the whole print on its fabric and nothing else, never enlarged, retouched or recolored. The home page and the product page’s design story show it. It is not part of the product gallery.
- **Plates.** Each piece’s surface is also rendered alone, as a wide and a tall field (`plate-wide.webp`, `plate-tall.webp`), with the listing image's garment cut out on its own (`piece.webp`): cut from the shop's first photo itself, with that photo's own shadow, so the home page shows exactly what the shop leads with (a tee's sleeves that run off the photo are completed, a hoodie's faded at the photo's edge; Fourthwall's current photos show every garment whole). The home page opens on the pieces’ plates in turn and every product page sets its design story on one. A plate carries only lettering of 24px and up, filled buttons and images; small text stays on coal or bone. The ink on a plate is bone or coal, chosen from the rendered plate so lettering clears 3:1 (bone on oxblood, slate, sage, cornflower and crimson; coal on ochre, sandstone, rose, linen and concrete). Plate colors (`plate-*` tokens) are photography, not interface: never on links, borders, text or controls.
- Richard’s portrait (`apps/web/public/about/richard-*.webp`): captions and alt text name Richard and nothing he is wearing, and it is never presented as a photo of a product.
- The product stays the focal point: preserve the artwork and garment colors exactly; the listing image always shows the whole garment; artwork crops are secondary images; no props or textures over the print; no fog, dramatic effects, glows or white outlines; clean garment edges. Changing the page color doesn't fix an embedded white background: cut the garment out and composite it.
- Keep the source images intact. In the repo, `assets/mockups/fourthwall.json` lists each product's Fourthwall images in order and `fourthwall.py` downloads them; `assets/mockups/source` holds the approved mockups and `cutout.py` cuts them out; `compose.py` builds the site images (`1.webp`, `2.webp`, …), each piece’s close-up and its plate, and prints the contrast each plate gives bone and coal lettering.
- New apparel art: symbols only (above), engraved line work, bone, black, deep red and the occasional single accent ink (Stone in Motion's cobalt).

## Voice

Reverent, weighty, sure of itself: short sentences, concrete images from Scripture, no sermons, no exclamation marks, no hype words ("insane", "fire", "drip"), no fear or guilt ("Don't be lukewarm"). Sentence case in copy (buttons display caps through styling; button and selector labels may be title case, as in Explore Behold, Latest Drop). Emoji only in social captions, at most one.

- **Design and meaning get equal attention.** Talk about the art, lettering and garment plainly, and let the Scripture and its meaning carry the faith.
- **Three voices, kept apart.** What Awebound makes: plain statements about the clothes and where they come from. What the passage says: the exact quotation, and one sentence on what the art shows. What Richard intends: his own first person, under his name. New marketing copy is functional (where a button goes, what can be ordered).
- **Conversations about Jesus are Richard’s hope**, not the sales pitch. Say it as his intention (“artwork that… opens the door to conversations about Jesus Christ”). Don’t tell wearers what they will say, and don’t assume the reader believes.
- **Bound in awe**: held by who He is, letting reverence for Jesus Christ shape what we make and wear. **Worn without shame**: not ashamed of the gospel.
- **Founder story**: Richard’s own words, as he supplied them in October 2026, and nothing invented beyond them. The home page carries his short note under the heading “My faith in God comes first.”: “I started Awebound with a desire to serve Christ and others through clothing rooted in Scripture. I want to create pieces people love wearing, with artwork that reflects biblical stories and opens the door to conversations about Jesus Christ.” Its link reads **Read my story**. About carries his full story (`STORY` in `apps/web/src/app/about/page.tsx`): he serves his community in law enforcement and leads a men’s Bible study; he believes we are saved by grace through faith in Jesus Christ, and his desire to serve Him comes from gratitude for that grace; he is drawn to expressive lettering, oversized fits and artwork that tells a story; Behold is the beginning of the work; and whoever comes for the artwork, the meaning behind it or simply to learn more is welcome. Keep his beliefs in his first person. Never add a testimony, struggle, rank, place, congregation or quotation; never turn his convictions into claims about the visitor; never tie a spiritual promise to buying; and don’t wrap his story in quotation marks or set it like Scripture. Ask Richard for anything more.
- **Sound like a person.** Active voice, plain words, contractions where they're natural, short sentences, concrete detail from the passage. Cut filler, stacked hedges, "not X, but Y" turns, colon-led one-liners, lists of three used for rhythm, and summing-up lines at the end of a paragraph. Write for anyone curious about Awebound, Christian or not, and not for search engines. US English.
- Say the actual cut of each product (tee, oversized tee, hoodie, tank, cap); never describe the whole brand as one fit.
- **My Archive** is where a piece and its meaning continue once it's yours. The account is free and never needed to order. A design's deeper story, Richard's note and its extras (phone and desktop wallpapers, the design card PDF, story images) open with a paid order linked to the account, kept with its Scripture and biblical context; signing up alone opens nothing. Scripture, biblical context and product descriptions stay open to everyone. Every explanation (sign-in, home page, product page, welcome, FAQ) names all of this wherever accounts are open, the owner's choice so the site explains the whole Archive while its last back-end steps are finished; what a person actually gets still follows what works. The site words every claim in one place, `apps/web/src/features/archive/offer.ts`. No membership, exclusivity, guilt or pressure language. Mention the Founding Member seal quietly, only while places remain, and never count the places left.

## Before you hand work back

- Wordmark from the file or component, intact, at least 120px wide, with clear space of a quarter of its height; no font imitating it.
- Only brand tokens. Text contrast at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for lettering of 24px and up on a plate); oxblood never used as text on coal. One primary action per screen (oxblood on coal; the plate’s ink on a plate). No glows, gradients or blur. Corners 0–2px, no pills.
- Grenze Gotisch only for display; Cormorant Garamond Italic only for quotations; Manrope for everything else; body 16–18px.
- No internal codes visible; no "streetwear"; no garment/color subtitles where the Scripture belongs.
- Every quotation is exact NIV with a matching label (and "excerpt" when partial), wraps in full, and agrees across home, shop and product page. Printed art isn't labeled NIV unless it uses NIV wording. The Biblica notice is present wherever NIV is quoted.
- No not-ready wording anywhere (no “ordering isn’t open”, “coming soon” or preview notices), no launch dates, and the supplier never named to customers.
- No small label above a heading anywhere, no status chips, no middle-dot metadata strings, no decorative numbering (About’s I, II, III are a real sequence), and nothing animating on load or on scroll (the home page’s pieces taking turns aside). Nothing but large lettering, filled buttons and images on a plate.
- Nothing tells the visitor what to believe or who the clothes are for; Richard’s convictions stay in his first person.
- Symbols-only imagery; styled product images with no white backgrounds, whole garment first.
- Copy has no exclamation marks, hype, fear or guilt, no invented founder details, and never names what Richard wears in the portrait.

## Keeping this skill in sync

`docs/BRAND.md` in the repo is a copy of this file: change both together. `assets/tokens.css`, `assets/components/` (CSS, ESM and UMD bundles, `index.d.ts`), `assets/tailwind-theme.css`, `assets/tailwind-preset.js`, `assets/logos/` and `assets/marks/` come from `packages/brand`. After the site's brand code changes, run `node .claude/skills/awebound-brand/scripts/sync-from-site.mjs` from the repo; it rebuilds them and reports any token missing from `references/tokens.json`. The Markdown references and the token usage notes are written by hand: update them when a rule, page or piece changes.
